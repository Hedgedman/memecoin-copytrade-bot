# Review 2026-10-06: is the Solana live bot worth running?

Data: snapshots of `~/Documents/copybot-live/copybot.db` (live) and `copybot-dev/copybot.db` (paper),
both taken 2026-10-06 20:20 (read-only), plus the live wallet's on-chain history
(`<live-wallet>`, 111 transactions, public RPC, read-only). No code or config was changed.
All 52 live positions were closed at the snapshot.

## Verdict: (c) stop live trading

**There is no edge in this data, and the losses are structural, not bad luck.**

- Live lost **−1.761 SOL on 52 trades (−6.8% per trade)** in 22.6 hours. That is about −1.9 SOL a day at this pace.
  Every period lost money: H2 −0.80, H3 −0.89. Without the 3 best trades the result is −3.63 SOL.
- Paper confirms it on a larger sample: **126 trades, −4.0% per trade** (bootstrap P(mean > 0) = 4%).
- The cause is visible in every cut of the data:
  - We buy a **median 13.9% above the leader's price** (average +20.6%).
  - We start **−3% down** just from fees and price impact.
  - Stops fill **5–6 points worse** than configured.
  - The average token **falls 17% within 30 min and 21% within 60 min** after our fill, in live and in paper.
  - Profit depends on catching rare runners. No exit rule did that consistently.
- None of the exit candidates (A–E) wins in both live and paper once the single best trade is removed.
  No subset (leader, market cap, venue, hour) is profitable with an adequate sample.
- Proving even a real +5%/trade edge needs about **240 trades** (σ ≈ 39% per trade). At the current live loss rate,
  collecting that live would cost about 8 SOL. Paper collects it for free.

**Recommendation:**
1. Stop the live bot.
2. Reclaim the 0.065 SOL of token-account rent (43 empty accounts).
3. Keep paper running.
4. If you want a Hypothesis 4, pre-register it **on paper only**: drop `Leader-G`, fixed TP +30% / stop −20%
   (the most stable style, ~0% everywhere).
5. Go live again only if it is clearly positive on ≥150 *new* paper trades. Set that threshold before looking.

## 2. Execution reality

| | configured | realised (exit vs cost) | trigger vs reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop, IDs 1–30 (25 stops) | −10% | avg **−16.7%**, median −13.5%, worst −48.3% | −14.9% |
| Stop, IDs 31–52 (15 stops) | −20% | avg **−25.5%**, median −23.2%, worst −39.5% | −23.0% |

- Two things make stops worse than configured:
  - The stop measures from the first exit quote, which is already ~3.5% below cost (fees and impact).
  - Prices jump past the level between pool updates.
- 10 of 25 (−10% era) and 5 of 15 (−20% era) stops ended more than 5 points beyond the setting.
- The 3 failed sells were all stops (slippage error) and ended at −22.7%, −33.9% and −39.5%.
- **Entry vs leader:** our cost per token vs the leader's on the same buy (n = 52): **median +13.9%, average +20.6%**,
  p25 +1.6%, p75 +33.0%, max +91.1%. Fees and rent explain under 1% of this. The rest is buying after his buy
  (and other copiers) has already moved the price.
- **Speed is not the issue.** We fill a median 2 slots behind the leader (p75 3, max 10), 1.06 s median from
  notification to fill. Exits fill 0.88 s after the trigger (median). Being faster can't remove a +14% gap that comes
  from his own price impact.
- **Failures:** 2 failed buys (same token, slippage), 3 failed stop sells (each made the loss worse).

## 3. Exit candidates

  price as in the bot.
- Live settings: 0.9 s exit lag (measured) and 0.0003 SOL per sell (measured).
- Paper settings: the configured delay (1.5 s before the restart, 0.8 s after) and 0.01 SOL per sell.
- Only positions with ticks are included (live H3: 19 of 22, live all: 32 of 52, paper: 71 of 127).

- IDs 1–30: −10.5% simulated vs −10.8% actual.

## 4. Signal quality

To judge signals without any exit rule, I measured each position's value at +5 / +30 / +60 min after our fill,
from the ticks (fees and price impact included): live 32 positions, paper 71.

| | n | mean | 95% CI | median | share > 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +30 min, paper | 52 | −16.7% | −30 .. −4% | −13.8% | 37% |
| +30 min, live | 23 | −17.1% | −35 .. +1% | −13.6% | 39% |
| +60 min, paper | 37 | −21.6% | −36 .. −6% | −18.5% | 27% |
| +60 min, live | 17 | −20.0% | −40 .. −2% | −18.9% | 29% |

37% of tokens peak at ≥ +50% within the hour after our fill (17% at ≥ +100%), then most give it back.
On average the copied buy is close to the local top. That makes profit an exit-timing problem, and part 3 shows
no rule solves it consistently.

|---|---|---|---|---|

- Even allowing for testing ~20 groups, Leader-G is the one result whose interval is far from zero.
  It is consistent in live and paper.
- These are his own trades, not fee-share receipts misread as buys. The bot only copies transactions the leader
  signs, and the copied transaction I could fetch on-chain was signed by him plus the FOMO co-signer `AgmLJBMD`.
  (Public-RPC throttling blocked the other 10.)
- No subset is clearly profitable. Everything that looks positive has fewer than 20 trades, or depends on one
  or two runners.
- With σ ≈ 39% per trade, a subset needs well over 100 trades before a +5–10% edge can be told apart from noise.
  The data is too thin for leader-level or hour-level conclusions.

## 5. Verdict, with numbers

**(c) Stop live trading.**

| evidence | number |
|---|---|
| Live result | −1.761 SOL / 52 trades, −6.8% per trade (95% CI −15.8% .. +5.8%; P(mean > 0) ≈ 12%) |
| Paper result (wider filters, old exits) | −4.0% per trade over 126 trades (95% CI −8.3% .. +0.4%; P(mean > 0) ≈ 4%) |
| Entry price vs leader | median +13.9% (structural, not a speed problem: 2 slots, ~1 s) |
| Instant round-trip cost | −2.6% median |
| Stop slippage | −10% → −16.7% realised; −20% → −25.5% realised |
| Token path after our fill | −17% at 30 min, −21% at 60 min (live and paper agree) |
| Dependence on runners | without the top 3 trades: −3.63 SOL |
| Best exit style across all sets | B, ~0% (break-even at best) |
| Trades needed to prove a +5% edge | ~240; at the live loss rate that costs ~8 SOL to learn live |

Why not (a): the current settings (A) are negative in every live and paper set.

Why not (b): no candidate passes the "wins in both" test. Making B live as H4 would be trading on a break-even
hypothesis with real money and real slippage. A hypothesis this weak belongs on paper.

**What would change this:** a pre-registered H4, run on paper only and judged on ≥150 new trades, that is clearly
positive with the single best trade removed. Suggested H4 for paper:
- Drop `Leader-G`.
- Fixed TP +30% / stop −20%.
- mc 30k–500k.

Changing paper strategy is your call: CLAUDE.md says not to change copybot-v2's strategy.
